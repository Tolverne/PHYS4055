====== PAGE 15 ======
Gaussian mode


 A plane wave extends infinitely in the transverse direction and is therefore an idealization.

 Real laser beams have a finite transverse width.

 The simplest model of a finite-width laser beam is a Gaussian mode.

 The beam is narrowest at the beam waist.

 The wavefront is approximately flat near the waist.

====== PAGE 20 ======
Why introduce coherent states?


 Number states describe states with an exact photon number:

                                   ˆn|n⟩= n|n⟩


 However, a number state has no definite optical phase and its average electric field is zero:
                                       ⟨n| ˆE(t)|n⟩= 0


 Coherent states provide the closest quantum description of a classical electromagnetic wave.

 An ideal, stable laser field is approximately described by a coherent state.

 In a coherent state,

                          ⟨α| ˆE(t)|α⟩= E0 ! αe−iωt + α∗eiωt"

 Equivalently,
                          ⟨ˆE(t)⟩= 2E0|α| cos(ωt −arg α)                                                                          20

====== PAGE 29 ======
Summary

The electromagnetic field can be described as a collection of independent modes.

Each mode is quantized as a harmonic oscillator:

                                            1
                                ˆH =       +                                   ℏωj   ˆnj                                            2                          "    #           !j


 Coherent states are eigenstates of the annihilation operator:

                                  ˆa|α⟩= α|α⟩

 Coherent states have a nonzero mean electric field and most closely resemble classical light.
