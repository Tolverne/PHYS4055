
\section*{Part IV. Applications of atomic physics \hfill 14 / 15}
\subsection*{1. Why an atom can test fundamental physics}
Electronic energies and transition amplitudes are calculable
using atomic structure theory and can be measured with very
high precision. Comparing them tests electromagnetic theory,
the weak interaction and possible physics beyond the Standard
Model. Week 12 develops two complementary examples:
atomic parity violation (APV) and electric dipole moments (EDMs).

\subsection*{2. Atomic parity violation: weak interactions mix parity}
Under parity \(P\), \(\mathbf r\to-\mathbf r\).
The electric dipole operator \(\mathbf d=-e\mathbf r\) is
parity-odd, so an E1 matrix element between same-parity
states vanishes when parity is conserved.
The weak neutral-current interaction contains
parity-violating electron--nucleus couplings, so
\[
H=H_{\rm EM}+H_{\rm weak},\qquad
\ket{\tilde i}\simeq\ket i+
\sum_{n\ne i}\ket n
\frac{\langle n|H_{\rm weak}|i\rangle}{E_i-E_n}.
\]
To first order, two nominally same-parity states may acquire
a small parity-violating E1 amplitude:
\[
\boxed{E_{\rm PV}\simeq
\sum_n\!\left[
\frac{\langle f|d|n\rangle\langle n|H_W|i\rangle}{E_i-E_n}
+\frac{\langle f|H_W|n\rangle\langle n|d|i\rangle}{E_f-E_n}
\right].}
\]
The weak charge is approximately
\(Q_W\simeq -N+Z(1-4\sin^2\theta_W)\)
at tree level, with electroweak radiative corrections and
nuclear-structure details needed for precision predictions.
APV experiments combine precision spectroscopy,
electronic-structure theory and weak-interaction physics.

\subsection*{3. EDMs probe \(P\)- and \(T\)-violating physics}
An intrinsic permanent electric dipole aligned with angular
momentum reverses under parity and (for spin alignment)
under time reversal. In an applied electric field,
\[
\boxed{H_{\rm EDM}=-\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}.}
\]
Comparing spin/precession energy under reversal of
electric and magnetic fields seeks an EDM-like shift.
A nonzero fundamental EDM at an accessible scale
could indicate CP-violating physics beyond the Standard Model,
subject to the relationship between \(T\) and CP symmetries.
Schiff screening suppresses the electric field at
point-like constituents of an ideal nonrelativistic neutral
atom in electrostatic equilibrium. Relativistic effects,
finite nuclear size and molecular enhancement allow
sensitive electron/nuclear EDM measurements.

\begin{methodbox}
\textbf{Exam method:} determine whether the operator is even/odd
under \(P\) or \(T\); identify the small perturbation;
use first-order state mixing and a matrix element.
Explain what observable is measured and why a symmetry-forbidden
background is suppressed.
\end{methodbox}
