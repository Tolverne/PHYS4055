                       Question 1: Two atoms coupled to a common cavity mode


  Two identical two-level atoms are resonantly coupled, with equal coupling strength g, to the same cavity mode. In
the interaction picture, the Tavis–Cummings Hamiltonian is
                                        (         )
                                                   ˆHI = ℏg   ˆa ˆJ+ + ˆa† ˆJ−   ,

where

                                                       ˆJ± = ˆσ(1)± + ˆσ(2)±  .

  The cavity initially contains no photons, atom 1 is excited and atom 2 is in its ground state:

                                        |Ψ(0)⟩= |e, g; 0⟩.

  Define the symmetric and antisymmetric single-excitation states

                                                       |e, g⟩+ |g, e⟩                |e, g⟩−|g, e⟩
                          |S⟩=   √          ,    |A⟩=   √          .
                                           2                     2

   1. Write the initial atomic state |e, g⟩as a superposition of |S⟩and |A⟩.

   2. Show that
                            √
                                      ˆJ−|S⟩=   2|g, g⟩,    ˆJ−|A⟩= 0.

   3. Explain why the symmetric state interacts with the cavity while the antisymmetric state does not.

   4. The symmetric component evolves according to
                           √         √
                                         |S; 0⟩−→cos(  2gt)|S; 0⟩−i sin(  2gt)|g, g; 1⟩,

     while |A; 0⟩does not evolve. Write the complete atom–cavity state at time t.

   5. Calculate the expected number of photons in the cavity,

                                                    ⟨ˆncav⟩= ⟨ˆa†ˆa⟩,

     and the expected total number of excited atoms,
                                       ⟨             ⟩
                                         ⟨ˆNat⟩=   ˆσ(1)+  ˆσ(1)−+ ˆσ(2)+  ˆσ(2)−     .

      Verify that the total number of excitations is conserved.

   6. What is the maximum probability of finding one photon in the cavity? Explain why the initial atomic excitation
     cannot be transferred completely to the cavity.

   7. Calculate the probability that each atom is excited as a function of time. Show that, at an appropriate time,
     the excitation is transferred completely from atom 1 to atom 2.
