====== PAGE 20 ======
Why do we need to quantize light?

Black-body radiation: the first step towards quantized light

In 1900 Planck (motivated by this
experiment by Lummer and Pringsheim)
found that black-body radiation could only
be correctly modelled by assuming that
atomic light emission was quantized

Planck reproduced the observations by
assuming that electromagnetic
oscillators exchange energy in discrete
amounts:

              𝐸! = 𝑛ℏ𝜔.

This assumption marked the beginning
of quantum theory.

Summary


   Experiments showed that classical physics cannot fully explain light.

    Classically, light is described using electric and magnetic fields that obey Maxwell’s equations.


   Boundary conditions in an optical cavity select a discrete set of electromagnetic modes.


   Each mode behaves like a harmonic oscillator.


   The excitations of a quantized electromagnetic mode are photons:
