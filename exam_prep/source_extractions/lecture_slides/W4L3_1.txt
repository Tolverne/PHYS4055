   The one-time pad is secure as longs as:

          1. The secret key is at least as long as the plain text

          2. Key must be random

          3. Key can never be reused

          4. Key must be completely secret

BB84 – 3. Key distillation


                                                                                   In the key distillation step (also known as
     Alice’s sifted key  0    1    1    0    1    0    0    1    0
                                                                                  error correction) Alice and Bob compare a
     Bob’s sifted key 0    1    0    0    1    1    0    1    0         subset of their bits from the sifted key to
                                                                         estimate the fraction of errors, called
                                                               quantum bit error rate (QBER).


If they see errors, this signals the presence of an eavesdropper.

An intercept-resend attack would introduce a QBER of 25%. However, Eve could have better technology and perform
different attacks that introduce less error. Alice and Bob never know the causes of errors; therefore, they must attribute
all errors to Eve (even if they are caused by environmental noise).

A secure key can be extracted even in the presence of error! If Alice and Bob estimate that they share more information
with each other than Eve could have obtained, they proceed with the rest of the protocol. First, they correct their keys to
remove errors using classical statistics methods.

====== PAGE 29 ======
BB84 – 4. Privacy amplification

 After the key distillation step, Alice’s and Bob’s keys are identical.

 Eve still has some information on the key.

 At this point, Alice and Bob perform privacy amplification: they use classical algorithms to decrease Eve’s information on
 the key, at the cost of discarding key bits. Hashing could be used for privacy amplification as well. They do this until they
 are reasonably sure that Eve does not have any information on the key at all.

 The key they share after privacy amplification is the secret key.

Quantum key distribution – entanglement based

Alice                                                                            Bob


         •  Alice and Bob can also share a secret key using an ‘entanglement based’ QKD scheme.
         •  Many pairs of entangled qubits are distributed between Alice and Bob.
         •  They agree to measure a subset of the qubits in prearranged bases, either { 0  , |1⟩} or { +  , |−⟩}, chosen
           at random and compare results.
         •    If the measurement outcomes are perfectly correlated, then they know the channel is secure and there
             is no eavesdropper.
         •  This is equivalent to the ideal prepare-and-measure BB84 scheme.
