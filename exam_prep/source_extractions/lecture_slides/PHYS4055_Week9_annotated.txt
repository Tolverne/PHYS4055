                 Isotope shift

Consider an atom with a nucleus with protons Z and neutron N. There is often more than 1
possible isotope that may be experimentally studied. The isotope shift is the diﬀerence in the
energies of the states between an isotope with neutrons N' and that with neutrons N.


There are two sources of this difference: the difference in the masses of the nuclei; and the difference
in the volume of the nuclei.


Here, we will consider the most signiﬁcant of these effects for heavy atoms — the "volume" or "ﬁeld"
shift. This change in the volume may be parametrized by the differential mean square nuclear charge
