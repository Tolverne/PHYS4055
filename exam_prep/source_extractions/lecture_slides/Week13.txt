====== PAGE 18 ======
The second is defined based on the Cs clock

• Original definition: 1⁄86,400 of a mean solar day
• 1956: The second was redefined in terms of a year. The second was thus defined
  as "the fraction ​1⁄31,556,925.9747 of the tropical year for 1900 January 0 at 12
  hours ephemeris time"
• 1967: The second is the duration of 9192631770 periods of the radiation
  corresponding to the transition between the two hyperfine levels of the ground
  state of the caesium-133 atom.
• 2019 definition: The second, symbol s, is the SI unit of time. It is defined by taking
  the fixed numerical value of the caesium frequency ΔνCs, the unperturbed
  ground-state hyperfine transition frequency of the caesium-133 atom, to
 be 9192631770 when expressed in the unit Hz, which is equal to s−1.

====== PAGE 32 ======
• Fundamental constant: any parameter that cannot be
  calculated with our present knowledge of physics, i.e.,
 a free parameter of a current theory*

• Examples: electron charge (e), electron mass     proton                                                (me),
 mass (mp), Plank constant (ħ), velocity of light in vacuum
  (c), Newton constant (G), Avogadro constant (NA),
 Boltzmann constant (kB), and the permeability and
  permittivity of space (ε0 and µ0)

• The number of free parameters depends on the physical
 model at hand (for example, the minimal standard model of
  particle physics plus gravitation depends on 20 free
  parameters)

* J-P.Uzan, Reviews of Modern Physics 75, 403 (2003)

====== PAGE 34 ======
Are fundamental constants constant?


 Various theories and extensions of the Standard Model
predict varying fundamental constants.
    •  Extra space dimensions are a common feature of theories unifying
      gravity with other interactions (Kaluza-Klein, Superstring and M-
      theories). Any change in size of these dimensions would manifest
       itself in the 3D world as variation of fundamental constants

     • Fundamental constants depend on scalar fields, which vary in
     space and time. May be related to “dark energy” and accelerated
     expansion of the Universe.

====== PAGE 35 ======
• Anthropic principle (Goldilock principle):
“...conditions that are observed in the universe must allow the observer to
  exist.”*
 Such “fine tuning” of physical laws, necessary for humans to exist, places constraints on
  the values of fundamental constants.


Example:  low-energy  resonance  in  production  of  carbon  from  helium  in  stars
  (He+He+He=>C). Slightly different (a few percent) fine structure constant α≡e2/ħc:
  unstable carbon nuclei→ no life


      Variation of coupling constants in space provides a natural explanation of the “fine
  tuning”: we appeared in area of the Universe where values of fundamental constants
  are suitable for our existence (Goldilocks principle).
