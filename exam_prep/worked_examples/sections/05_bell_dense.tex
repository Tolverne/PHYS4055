
\section{OA-01: Bell states, partial traces and dense coding}
\source{2026 Optics Assignment 2, Questions 1--2; released solutions.}
\subsection*{Step 1: build the joint density matrix}
For \(\ket{\Phi^+}=(\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt2\),
\[
\rho_{AB}=\ket{\Phi^+}\bra{\Phi^+}
=\frac12(\ket{00}\bra{00}+\ket{00}\bra{11}
+\ket{11}\bra{00}+\ket{11}\bra{11}).
\]
Tracing over Bob uses
\(\operatorname{Tr}_B(\ket{i,j}\bra{k,l})
=\delta_{jl}\ket i\bra k\).
The cross terms disappear:
\[
\boxed{\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B\rho_{AB}
=\tfrac12(\ket0\bra0+\ket1\bra1)=\tfrac12I}.
\]
Thus Alice's measurement is \(0\) or \(1\) with probability
\(1/2\), even though the two-qubit state is pure.
\(\operatorname{Tr}\rho_A^2=\tfrac12<1\) confirms mixing.

\subsection*{Step 2: rewrite the correlations in another basis}
With \(\ket\pm=(\ket0\pm\ket1)/\sqrt2\),
substitute \(\ket0=(\ket++\ket-)/\sqrt2\),
\(\ket1=(\ket+-\ket-)/\sqrt2\). The cross terms cancel:
\[
\boxed{\ket{\Phi^+}
=\frac{\ket{++}+\ket{--}}{\sqrt2}}.
\]
The outcomes are individually random but perfectly
correlated in both the \(Z\) and \(X\) bases.

\subsection*{Step 3: superdense coding}
Alice and Bob already share \(\ket{\Phi^+}\).
Alice acts on \emph{her} qubit:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\text{Message}&\text{Alice's gate}&\text{shared state}\\\hline
00&I&\ket{\Phi^+}\\
01&X&\ket{\Psi^+}\\
10&Z&\ket{\Phi^-}\\
11&XZ&-\ket{\Psi^-}
\end{array}
\]
The last minus is an irrelevant global phase. The
four Bell states are mutually orthogonal: e.g.,
\(\langle\Phi^+|\Phi^-\rangle=(1-1)/2=0\).
After Alice sends her photon, an ideal Bell-basis
measurement at Bob identifies two classical bits.

\subsection*{Step 4: why ordinary linear optics is limited}
At a passive 50:50 splitter, bosonic interference makes
\(\Psi^-\) and \(\Psi^+\) distinguishable using appropriate
port/polarisation coincidence patterns, but
\(\Phi^+\) and \(\Phi^-\) share the same unresolved
patterns under the standard passive scheme.
Consequently only two of four equally likely Bell
messages are unambiguously identified:
\[
\boxed{P_{\rm unambiguous}=\tfrac24=\tfrac12}.
\]
This is a measurement limitation, not a failure of dense
coding with an ideal Bell measurement.
\checkmethod{A reduced mixed state alone does not prove that
the joint state is mixed. The two independent random local
strings can still be strongly correlated.}
