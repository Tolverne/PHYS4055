Question 1: Entanglement distillation

Alice and Bob share n copies of the mixed state

                                                              1
                 ρAB = p ϕ+  ϕ+ + (1 −p) ψ+  ψ+   ,    p >    ,
                                                              2

where
                          1                          1
               ϕ+ = √  (|00⟩+ |11⟩) ,    ψ+ = √  (|01⟩+ |10⟩)
                          2                          2
are Bell states. The goal of entanglement distillation is to use several imperfectly entangled
pairs to obtain fewer pairs with higher entanglement.
   For one step of the protocol, Alice and Bob take two copies of ρAB. On each side, they apply
a CNOT gate, using the qubit from the first pair as the control qubit and the qubit from the
second pair as the target qubit. The unitary operation of the CNOT gate can be written as

                       ˆUCNOT = |0C⟩⟨0C| ⊗ˆIT + |1C⟩⟨1C| ⊗ˆσx,T ,

that is, the target qubit is flipped if and only if the control qubit is |1⟩.
   After applying the two CNOT gates, Alice and Bob measure the target pair in the compu-
tational basis {|0⟩, |1⟩} as shown in the figure below (where the first line is the target and the
second line is the control). If they obtain the same measurement outcome, they keep the control
pair; otherwise, they discard it.





  (a) Calculate the state after one successful step:

     (a.1) Calculate the initial joint system.

     (a.2) Apply the CNOT gates.

     (a.3) Measure in the described basis and determine the resulting state.

 (b) What is the probability that one distillation step is successful?

  (c) Calculate the fidelity of the resulting state with respect to |ϕ+⟩.  Is it larger or smaller
     than the initial fidelity?

 (d) Suppose that, after one successful step, Alice and Bob repeat the same procedure using
      pairs with the improved fidelity. What do you expect to happen to the fidelity after several
      successful rounds? What is the trade-off?

   Solution

(a.1) We label the two copies as pair 1 and pair 2, which correspond to control and target pairs,
      respectively. The two-copy state is
                         ρ⊗2AB = p2 ϕ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 ϕ+ 1 ϕ+ 2
                    + p(1 −p) ϕ+  ψ+   ϕ+  ψ+                                                          1      2      1      2
                    + p(1 −p) ψ+  ϕ+  ψ+  ϕ+                                                          1      2      1      2
                    + (1 −p)2 ψ+  ψ+  ψ+  ψ+    .                                                          1      2      1      2

     This is a mixed state that yields the four possible two-pair input states with probabilities

                                    input state  probability
                                     |ϕ+⟩1 |ϕ+⟩2       p2
                                    |ϕ+⟩1 |ψ+⟩2    p(1 −p)
                                   |ψ+⟩1 |ϕ+⟩2    p(1 −p)
                                   |ψ+⟩1 |ψ+⟩2    (1 −p)2

(a.2) Now Alice applies a CNOT from her qubit in pair 1 (control) to her qubit in pair 2 (target),
     and Bob does the same on his side. We can track separately what happens to the four
      possible Bell-state combinations, keeping in mind that Alice operates on the first qubit of
     each Bell state, and Bob on the second qubit. If the control pair is |a1b1⟩1 and the target
      pair is |a2b2⟩2, the bilateral CNOT acts as

                              |a1b1⟩1 |a2b2⟩2 7−→|a1b1⟩1 |a1 ⊕a2, b1 ⊕b2⟩2 .

    We now apply this to the four possible input states:

                                  1
         ˆUCNOT ϕ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 = ˆUCNOT  (|00⟩1 |00⟩2 + |00⟩1 |11⟩2 + |11⟩1 |00⟩2 + |11⟩1 |11⟩2)                                  2
                            1
                 =   (|00⟩1 |00⟩2 + |00⟩1 |11⟩2 + |11⟩1 |11⟩2 + |11⟩1 |00⟩2)                            2
                 = ϕ+  ϕ+     ,                                         1      2


     and similarly
                             ˆUCNOT ϕ+ 1 ψ+ 2 = ϕ+ 1 ψ+ 2 ,
                             ˆUCNOT ψ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 = ψ+ 1 ψ+ 2 ,
                             ˆUCNOT ψ+ 1 ψ+ 2 = ψ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 .

(a.3) Alice and Bob measure the target pair in the computational basis. A successful event
     corresponds to Alice and Bob obtaining the same result, i.e., the target pair is projected
     onto either |00⟩2 or |11⟩2. The corresponding projection operator is
                           Πs = ˆI1 ⊗(|00⟩2 ⟨00|2 + |11⟩2 ⟨11|2) .

     Applying this projection to the four cases after the CNOT gives

                             Πs ϕ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 = ϕ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 ,
                             Πs ϕ+ 1 ψ+ 2 = 0 ,
                             Πs ψ+ 1 ψ+ 2 = 0 ,
                             Πs ψ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 = ψ+ 1 ϕ+ 2 .

    After the measurement, the target pair is discarded.  Therefore, the surviving control-
    pair states are |ϕ+⟩1 and |ψ+⟩1. Since the surviving contributions came from the initial
    components |ϕ+⟩1 |ϕ+⟩2 and |ψ+⟩1 |ψ+⟩2, with probabilities p2 and (1 −p)2, respectively,
    the unnormalised state of the retained pair is

                                ρ′unnorm = p2 ϕ+  ϕ+ + (1 −p)2 ψ+  ψ+  .

    Normalising, the state after one successful step is

                                   p2 |ϕ+⟩⟨ϕ+| + (1 −p)2 |ψ+⟩⟨ψ+|
                                  ρ′AB =                                                    .                                             p2 + (1 −p)2


(b) The probability of success is given by the coefficients of the unnormalised output state:

                                    Ps = p2 + (1 −p)2.

(c) The fidelity with respect to |ϕ+⟩is

                                                              ′                                              ′         p2                   F = ϕ+  ρ′AB ϕ+ = F =                      .                                                       p2 + (1 −p)2


    Since p > 1/2, this value is greater than the initial fidelity F = p. Therefore, one successful
     distillation step increases the fidelity with respect to |ϕ+⟩.

(d) If Alice and Bob repeat the protocol using two pairs with the same current fidelity pk,
    then after a successful step the fidelity becomes

                                                  p2k
                                  pk+1 =                        .
                                             p2k + (1 −pk)2

    Since in the initial state p0 > 1/2, the sequence increases towards 1. Thus, the retained
    pairs become closer and closer to the maximally entangled Bell state |ϕ+⟩.

   The trade-off is that the protocol consumes entangled pairs for measurements and only
    keeps the result when the measurement outcomes agree, which happens probabilistically.
    Therefore, the fidelity of the retained pairs increases, but the total number of remaining
    pairs decreases.
