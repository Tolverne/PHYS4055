\subsection*{1. Teleportation uses entanglement plus classical bits}
Alice holds an unknown qubit
\(\ket\psi=\alpha\ket0+\beta\ket1\) and one half of a
shared Bell pair; Bob holds the other half.
Alice performs a Bell-basis measurement and sends Bob
\emph{two classical bits}. Depending on her outcome, Bob's
state is one of \(\ket\psi,X\ket\psi,Z\ket\psi,XZ\ket\psi\)
(up to global phases). He applies the corresponding correction.
The original input is destroyed by Alice's measurement; there
is no faster-than-light information transfer.
\begin{methodbox}
\textbf{Exam method:} write \(\ket\psi\otimes\ket{\Phi^+}\);
apply CNOT (unknown qubit as control), then Hadamard to
the first qubit; regroup by Alice's two measurement bits.
Read off Bob's Pauli correction from each branch.
\end{methodbox}

\subsection*{2. Dense coding reverses the resource accounting}
If Alice and Bob share a Bell pair, Alice can apply one of
\(I,X,Z,XZ\) to her half, producing four distinguishable
Bell states. She sends \emph{one physical qubit} to Bob, who
performs a Bell measurement and recovers \emph{two bits}.
The shared entanglement is the essential extra resource.

\subsection*{3. BB84 quantum key distribution}
Alice encodes random bits in one of two conjugate bases,
such as \(\{\ket H,\ket V\}\) or
\(\{\ket+,\ket-\}\), and Bob chooses a random measurement
basis. They publicly announce bases, \emph{sift} positions
where bases match, estimate error rates using a disclosed
sample, then perform error correction and privacy
amplification. Security uses measurement disturbance and
no-cloning, not secrecy of the basis choices.
An intercept-resend eavesdropper choosing random BB84 bases
introduces a \(25\%\) error rate in the sifted bits in the ideal
single-photon model; real security analyses include losses,
multi-photon signals, finite-key effects and detector assumptions.

\subsection*{4. Continuous-variable QKD}
Rather than discrete photonic qubits, Alice can encode
information in optical quadratures; Bob measures them by
homodyne/heterodyne detection. An entanglement-based view
starts with a two-mode squeezed state and transmission through
a noisy/lossy channel. State covariance and channel parameters
then describe the observable correlations and security analysis.
\begin{cautionbox}
\textbf{Concept check:} teleportation transfers an unknown \emph{state};
dense coding transfers two \emph{classical bits};
QKD establishes a \emph{shared secret key}.
All have different resource requirements.
\end{cautionbox}
