What is quantum metrology?

Quantum metrology uses quantum systems as probes to estimate physical
parameters as precisely as possible.

                        1. Prepare the probe
                  Choose a quantum state of light


                             2. Encode the parameter
                     The parameter changes the probe


                                 3. Measure the probe
                         Record a measurement outcome


                                     4. Estimate the parameter
                                      Infer its value and uncertainty

Summary

        •  Fisher information determines precision: classical Fisher information
       depends on the measurement, while quantum Fisher information gives the
       maximum information permitted by the quantum state.

        • Quantum states can improve phase sensitivity: coherent light gives the
                                           1
        standard quantum limit Δ𝜙∝   &𝑛, while optimised nonclassical probes can
                                                  1
        approach Heisenberg scaling Δ𝜙∝ &𝑛.

        • Quantum metrology is used in gravitational-wave detection: LIGO
         converts differential arm-length changes into a dark-port signal and uses
        squeezed vacuum to reduce quantum noise.
